Woven or stitched?

The same alphabet, drawn once on a thread grid with strokes two threads wide, then made two ways. Bowa (বোৱা, woven) lays each row as a float of weft, like the motifs in a gamosa border. Siloi (চিলাই, stitched) makes every cell a cross-stitch, like a name embroidered on a shirt or a cushion.

There's an Assamese precedent for both: the Vrindavani Vastra, the cloths woven in Assam under Sankardev, carry verses in Assamese script woven into the fabric, and verses are sometimes embroidered onto gamosas too.

Close up

Woven letters are made of floats of doubled, twisted yarn: fine slanted grooves give the twist, a glint sits on each ply, the warp crosses over now and then as a fine line, and every thread casts a soft shadow on the cloth. The shading layers over the text colour, so it works in red, gold, cream or anything else. (Browsers that can't show colour fonts get the textured thread without the shading.)

How they compare

Woven (Bowa)Stitched (Siloi)
From across a roomSolid and bold. Reads like a woven label or a gamosa border.Lighter and airier. The letters shimmer, like embroidery.
Up closeYou see the floats of thread, row by row.You see every little X. Charming, handmade.
Small sizesHolds up better: rows merge into solid strokes.The crosses turn to grey texture; keep it large.
Ties to the brandDirectly: same grid, red and cream as the gamosa border.Through craft: home, care, something made by hand.
RiskCan look like a pixel font if set too small or in the wrong colours.Can look like a craft-shop font; needs restraint around it.
Best forThe logo, hero headlines, the rate board.Warm accents: a name, a badge, “Made in Tinsukia”.

They also work together: woven for the brand and headlines, stitched for small human touches, the way a gamosa can be woven and then embroidered.
